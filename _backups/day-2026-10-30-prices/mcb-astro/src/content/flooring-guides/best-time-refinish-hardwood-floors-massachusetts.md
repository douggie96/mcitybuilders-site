In Massachusetts, the best time to refinish hardwood floors is spring (roughly April to early June) or fall (September through October). Humidity is moderate, the boards sit close to their average size, and you can open windows to ventilate and help the finish dry. Winter works fine with the heat kept on, but expect seasonal gaps; summer humidity slows drying and can swell boards.

## Why spring and fall are the sweet spot

Wood moves with the moisture in the air, and in a New England house that swing is big. In January the furnace or wood stove dries the air and boards shrink; in a sticky August the same boards swell.

Spring and fall land in the middle. The finish goes down on boards that will not move much right away, and you can crack windows for fresh air without freezing the house or pulling in muggy air. That helps both drying and odor, which is why these windows book up first.

## Winter: it works, with a few catches

Winter is a perfectly good time to refinish, and it is often easier to get on a contractor's calendar. Know this first:

- **Gaps show up, then close.** Dry heat shrinks boards, so gaps you see in January often close on their own by summer. Filler packed into those gaps mid-winter usually gets squeezed and cracks out once the boards swell. We leave seasonal gaps alone and only fill permanent ones.
- **The house has to stay warm.** As one example, Bona's technical data sheet for its Traffic HD water-based finish lists a minimum of about 55°F and an optimal range of roughly 64–77°F at 30–60% relative humidity. Follow the label for your product, and keep the heat on, not set back, for the whole job.
- **Ventilation is harder.** You cannot leave windows open for hours in February. That matters most with oil-based polyurethane, which smells strong and dries slowly.

## Summer: humidity is the problem

Summer is the toughest season here. High humidity slows dry times between coats and can stretch the schedule. Boards that soak up moisture can cup (edges higher than the center), and sanding a cupped floor flat too early can leave it crowned once it dries back. If summer is your only option, run central air or a dehumidifier for several days before the job.

## Finish choice, new boards and older homes

**Water-based** finishes dry fast, have lower odor and stay clear. **Oil-based** polyurethane gives a warmer amber tone many people like on older oak, but dries slower and needs more ventilation. Either way, we usually apply three coats.

New boards, whether patching in or doing a full [hardwood floor installation](/flooring/hardwood-floor-installation), need to acclimate inside the house before they go down. Wood straight off a cold, damp truck in fall is how you get gaps and cupping later.

Plenty of homes in Harvard, Bolton and Lancaster have original wide-plank pine. It is soft, often face-nailed and full of character marks, so we sand it gently and treat some wear as part of the floor. On any older floor, the real question is how much wood is left above the tongue, since every full sanding removes some. We check at a floor register or threshold before quoting. If a floor has been sanded several times, a screen and recoat may be the smarter move. Engineered flooring often allows one sanding or none.

## How long it takes, and planning around it

- **Screen and recoat:** often a day.
- **Sand and clear finish, a room or two:** 3–4 days.
- **Stain plus oil-based finish on a larger area:** about a week.
- **After the last coat:** light sock traffic after about a day; furniture with felt pads after a few days; rugs wait about two weeks while the finish cures.

Do floors before you move in; an empty house is the easiest job. Avoid the week before Thanksgiving or a holiday party, so the finish is cured before guests and rugs arrive. If you are painting too, paint first and refinish last; our [interior painting](/interior-painting) crew can schedule ahead of the floor work.

With oil-based finish, most families stay with relatives or at a hotel for a few nights. With water-based, you may be able to live upstairs if there is another way in and out. Keep dogs and cats off the floor for several days, and trim claws before they come back.
