Lancaster Housing Stock — What We Run Into

Lancaster's housing stock is a mix that keeps a contractor honest. Along Main Street and Route 117 near the town green you have antique colonials and capes, some going back to the 1700s and 1800s, with post-and-beam frames, fieldstone foundations, plaster walls, and floor joists that were sized by eye rather than a span table. Remodeling a kitchen in one of these means expecting nothing to be level, plumb, or square, and building a budget that can absorb what we find behind the plaster.

South Lancaster and the streets off Route 70 and Route 62 have a lot of mid-century capes and ranches plus 1970s through 1990s colonials, and the newer subdivisions off Route 70 are mostly 2000s-era colonials on larger lots. Those houses have modern framing and are easier to open up, but they are hitting the age where original decks are rotting at the ledger, first-generation vinyl windows are failing, and builder-grade kitchens and baths are worn out.

We have done structural and rot repair work in Lancaster on sill plates, rim joists, and porch framing where decades of splash-back and poor grading did their damage. That kind of repair is rarely glamorous, but it is the work that keeps an old house standing, and it is usually cheaper to fix now than after the floor starts to drop.

What a General Contractor Does vs. Hiring Trades Separately

A general contractor takes responsibility for the whole job. On a Lancaster kitchen remodel that means demolition, framing changes, rough plumbing and electrical, insulation, drywall, tile, cabinets, counters, trim, paint, and final hookups, all scheduled in the right order and all under one contract with one price. We hire and manage the plumber and electrician, we schedule the Lancaster Building Department inspections at the right points, and we are the ones responsible if something is wrong.

Hiring trades separately can work if you have the time and the experience to act as your own GC. In practice, most homeowners run into the same problems: the electrician cannot start until the framer is done, the framer is on another job, the tile guy shows up before the plumber has moved the drain, and nobody wants to own the gap between trades. Every one of those handoffs is a place where the schedule slips and the cost creeps. You also end up pulling permits as a homeowner, which puts the liability on you.

With MCB you get one estimate, one contract, one schedule, and one phone number. Our own crew handles carpentry, tile, flooring, paint, and finish work. We bring in licensed plumbers and electricians for the rough-in and final connections. When the job is done, there is one person to call about anything, and that does not change after the check clears.

How We Work in Lancaster
