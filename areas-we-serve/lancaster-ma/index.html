ABOUT LANCASTER — WHAT THE HOUSING STOCK LOOKS LIKE

Lancaster is our home town and one of the oldest communities in Massachusetts, settled in 1653. That history shows up in the housing: the Main Street and Route 70 corridor and the streets around Lancaster Center are lined with antique colonials and farmhouses, including Federal and Greek Revival homes that predate 1900. Remodeling these houses is its own discipline — original framing, plaster walls, and period trim all have to be respected while the systems behind them are brought up to modern standard.

The town has several distinct pockets. South Lancaster, home to Atlantic Union College, mixes historic homes with steadier residential streets. North Village and the area near the Nashua River and the Bolton Flats sit lower and more rural. Newer construction — post-war capes and later subdivisions — spreads out along George Hill Road and Old Common Road, where lots are larger and better suited to additions and full-size decks. Each of these areas asks a slightly different question of a remodel, from tight antique footprints in the center to more open, modern layouts on the outskirts.

Many Lancaster homes outside the center are on private well and septic rather than municipal service. That matters for kitchen and bathroom work: drain routing, added fixtures, and any excavation for a deck footing all have to account for the septic system and leach field. We identify well and septic conditions during the estimate walk-through and build that reality into the plan, so a bath addition or a kitchen relocation does not run into a system conflict after demolition has already started.

Permits for remodeling in Lancaster are filed with the Lancaster Building Department at the Prescott Building, 701 Main Street. Most kitchen, bathroom, and deck projects need a building permit along with electrical and plumbing permits, and antique-home work sometimes brings additional structural review. Maverick City Builders prepares and files every application, coordinates the inspections, and keeps the paperwork moving so the project stays on schedule. Every Lancaster job is run under that supervision.
