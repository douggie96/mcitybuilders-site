ABOUT STERLING — WHAT THE HOUSING STOCK LOOKS LIKE

Sterling is a Worcester County town of roughly 8,000 people about 15 minutes north of our Lancaster shop, laid out along the Route 12, Route 62, and Route 140 corridors. The town has three main pockets of housing. Sterling Center, clustered around the town common, holds the oldest stock — antique colonials and farmhouses, many dating to the 18th and 19th centuries, mixed with early-1900s homes. West Sterling and the outlying rural stretches lean toward capes, ranches, and post-war construction, while Pratts Junction anchors the northern part of town near the old rail junction.

A defining feature of Sterling is its lake district. East Lake Waushacum and West Lake Waushacum are ringed with a mix of former seasonal cottages that have been converted to year-round homes, older ranches, and newer construction. These lakeside properties drive a steady stream of deck additions, kitchen updates, and full winterizing renovations as owners upgrade cottage-era homes to modern standards. Newer subdivisions off Route 12 and Route 62 fill out the town with 1980s-and-later colonials and capes on larger lots.

Because Sterling is largely rural, a high share of homes run on private well and septic rather than municipal water and sewer. A meaningful portion of the town also falls within the Wachusett Reservoir watershed, where septic siting, leach-field setbacks, and drain work carry extra scrutiny. That reality shapes how we plan bathroom, kitchen, and deck projects here: MCB confirms the location of the well, septic tank, and leach field during the estimate walk-through and builds supply and drain routing around them before work begins, rather than discovering a conflict mid-project.

Sterling carries real New England heritage — Davis Farmland and the historic 1741 Publick House sit within the town, and the common remains the civic heart. Permits for remodeling are filed through the Sterling Building Department at the Butterick Municipal Building, 1 Park Street. Standard residential permits for kitchen remodeling, bathroom remodeling, and deck construction are typically reviewed within one to three weeks. MCB prepares every application and schedules the inspections so homeowners never have to manage the permit process themselves.
