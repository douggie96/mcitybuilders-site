ABOUT HUDSON — WHAT THE HOUSING STOCK LOOKS LIKE

Hudson is a Middlesex County town of roughly 20,000 built along the Assabet River, once a shoe and mill town and now known for one of the most revitalized, walkable downtowns in the region. The award-winning Main Street and Wood Square corridor gives Hudson a real town center — something many of the rural towns around it lack — and that walkability shapes the housing right around the core. MCB works throughout Hudson, from the dense streets near the river to the newer subdivisions on the town's edges, and it sits about 20 minutes east of our Lancaster base.

Hudson's neighborhoods each have a distinct feel. The downtown and Wood Square area is anchored by Victorian and mill-era homes, many of them tight on small urban lots and originally built for shoe-factory workers. The Highlands and the residential streets around Houghton and Brigham hold a mix of early-1900s homes and colonials, and this is where we see a lot of full kitchen remodels and interior updates. Moving outward, dense 1900s through 1940s streets give way to mid-century ranches, and then to newer subdivisions closer to the Stow and Sudbury lines where lots are larger and backyards support deck projects.

Utilities in Hudson split by location. The downtown and older residential core is on municipal sewer and water, which keeps kitchen-drain and bathroom work straightforward. Homes on the outskirts — particularly toward the Stow, Bolton, and Sudbury borders — are more likely to be on private septic, so we confirm septic and leach-field locations during the estimate for any bath, kitchen-drain, or deck work near those systems. Identifying that up front keeps a project on schedule instead of stalling mid-build over a surprise.

Two project types dominate Hudson: updating the tight, dated kitchens common in the downtown-era housing near Wood Square, and adding a second bathroom to older one-bath homes that no longer fit the way families live. Permits for both are filed through the Hudson Building Department at Town Hall, 78 Main Street, where standard residential kitchen, bathroom, and deck permits are typically reviewed within one to three weeks. MCB handles all permit applications and inspection scheduling for Hudson projects, so the homeowner never has to chase paperwork at Town Hall.
