ABOUT CLINTON — WHAT THE HOUSING STOCK LOOKS LIKE

Clinton is the densest community in our Worcester County service area — a compact former textile-mill town about 12 minutes from our Lancaster shop. Where the surrounding towns spread out across farm lots and wooded acreage, Clinton is built on a tight downtown grid of narrow streets, small lots, and homes that sit close to one another and close to the road. That density shapes almost every project here: staging, dumpster placement, and material deliveries all have to be planned around narrow streets and shared driveways before the first day of work.

The housing stock skews older and tighter than anywhere else nearby. Downtown, along High Street, Church Street, and Union Street, you find late-1800s Victorians, two- and three-family homes, worker cottages, and duplexes originally built for the mill workforce. Greeley Hill and the Acre carry the same dense, walkable character. On the outskirts toward Route 110 and Route 62 the lots open up a little, with mid-century capes and ranches mixed into the older fabric. Because so much of Clinton was built for renters and multi-family living, investor and landlord remodels — unit turnovers and full building renovations — are a core part of the work we do here.

Unlike the rural towns we serve, most of Clinton is on municipal sewer rather than private septic. That simplifies drain and bathroom work — there is no leach field to plan around — but it does not make the older homes simple. Mill-era plumbing and early or knob-and-tube wiring are common in the downtown stock, and a serious kitchen or bath gut in a Victorian or three-decker often means bringing supply lines, drains, and electrical up to current code as part of the project. We flag those conditions during the estimate so the scope and the price account for them from the start, not halfway through the job.

Clinton is also home to the Wachusett Dam and the Clinton Reservoir, and the town's engineering heritage shows in its solidly built older housing. Permits for remodeling here are filed with the Clinton Building Department at Town Hall, 242 Church Street. Standard residential permits for kitchen remodeling, bathroom remodeling, and deck construction are typically reviewed in one to three weeks. MCB handles every permit application and schedules all inspections for Clinton projects, so homeowners and property owners are not the ones standing in line at Town Hall.
