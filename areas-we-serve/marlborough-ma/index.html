ABOUT MARLBOROUGH — WHAT THE HOUSING STOCK LOOKS LIKE

Marlborough is a city of about 40,000 at the intersection of Routes 20, 85, and 495 in MetroWest. The housing stock breaks into three main types: 1970s–1990s colonial subdivisions in established neighborhoods like French Hill, Hayden Heights, and Hayden Falls; mid-century ranches and split-levels in the older neighborhoods near downtown Main Street and Hosmer Street; and newer construction in developments near Williams Lake Road and the 495 interchange.

The colonial subdivisions — which make up the majority of Marlborough's housing stock — were built with standard 1980s finishes: oak cabinets, laminate countertops, fiberglass tub surrounds, and single-vanity bathrooms. These homes have strong bones and good layouts, but the finishes are now 30–40 years old. That's exactly where we operate: upgrading the finishes to match the home's actual value in today's market.

Marlborough's 01752 ZIP benefits from strong Route 495 commuter demand — buyers heading to Boston, Worcester, and the Route 128 tech corridor. This demand makes remodeling ROI in Marlborough stronger than comparable Worcester County towns. Updated kitchens and bathrooms consistently shorten time on market and support higher sale prices in this market.

Permits are filed with the Marlborough Building Department at City Hall, 140 Main Street. Standard residential permits — kitchen remodeling, bathroom remodeling, deck building — are typically approved in 1–2 weeks. We handle all permit applications and schedule all required inspections as part of every project.

Marlborough Housing Stock — What Remodeling Looks Like Here

Marlborough sits at the intersection of Central Massachusetts and the MetroWest corridor — which shows in its housing stock. Route 20 corridor homes range from 1950s and 1960s ranches and split-levels in the Bigelow, Hudson Street, and Maple Street neighborhoods to newer colonials in the Briarwood and Bolton Street subdivisions built through the 1990s and 2000s. The city also has a dense multi-family core near Downtown Marlborough and the old mill district along the Assabet River — housing stock comparable to Hudson's Washington Street corridor.

Ranches and split-levels in the Route 20 and Hosmer Street neighborhoods are ideal candidates for kitchen open-concept conversions — the wall between kitchen and family room is usually non-load-bearing and the footprint easily accommodates a 10-foot island. Bathrooms in this era have the same profile as Fitchburg and Leominster: original tub surrounds, small vanities, and cast-iron plumbing approaching the end of its serviceable life. Maverick City Builders handles all three tiers of Marlborough remodeling — the mid-century split-level gut, the newer subdivision cosmetic upgrade, and the downtown multi-family unit refresh. All permits through Marlborough Building Department at City Hall, 140 Main Street, all footings and structural work to code, all inspections walked by our crew.
