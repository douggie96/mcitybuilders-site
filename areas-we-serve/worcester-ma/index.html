ABOUT WORCESTER — WHAT THE HOUSING STOCK LOOKS LIKE

Worcester is New England's second-largest city and the seat of Worcester County, and its housing tells the story of an old industrial hub. The defining building type here is the classic three-decker — the stacked two- and three-family that lined the streets as the city grew around its factories a century ago. Around those you find sturdy Victorians, four-squares, and colonials spread across neighborhoods that each have their own character: Burncoat and Tatnuck to the north and west, the West Side and Elm Park near the parks and the college hill, Vernon Hill and Grafton Hill on the east and south sides, and the Shrewsbury Street and Union Hill area along the old restaurant row (Shrewsbury Street is a Worcester street name, not a separate town).

Most of this stock dates to the early 1900s and sits on small, tightly-packed lots served by municipal water and sewer — a very different context from the rural well-and-septic towns we cover farther north. That density shapes the work. Kitchens are narrow and closed-off, bathrooms are small, and the wiring, plumbing, and heating behind the plaster are frequently a mix of original and patched-in decades of updates. A serious Worcester remodel almost always means bringing part of the systems up to current code, not just swapping finishes, and we quote that reality up front instead of finding it after demolition.

Investor and multi-family work is a core project type for us in Worcester. Landlords turning over a unit in a three-decker, owners updating an owner-occupied floor while tenants stay put upstairs, and buyers renovating a whole two-family all need a contractor who can sequence work around occupied units and keep the job moving. On the West Side and around Elm Park the projects run larger — full colonial and Victorian renovations where original layouts get opened up, kitchens go open-concept, and mechanical systems are modernized floor by floor. Whatever the scope, the job is run on a written, fixed-price contract.

Permits for remodeling in Worcester are filed through the City of Worcester Inspectional Services Division at 25 Meade Street. Because the city's building stock is old and dense, plan review and inspections often look closely at electrical, plumbing, and egress — especially on multi-family properties. MCB pulls every permit and schedules all of the inspections, so Worcester homeowners and property owners never have to stand at the city counter or chase down a sign-off themselves. We build that permitting timeline into the schedule we give you before the first day of work.
