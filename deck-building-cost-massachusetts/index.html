That works out to roughly $34–$55 per square foot in pressure-treated and $55–$95 per square foot in composite for a 400 sq ft deck in Worcester County. Two things move a 20×20 outside these numbers faster than anything else: height and demolition. Raising the same deck to a second-story walkout adds $8,000–$18,000 for engineered attachment, taller posts, and a longer stair run. Composite framing costs slightly more than pressure-treated framing because most composite boards require joists at 12 inches on center instead of 16, and the hidden fastener system adds material and install time.

COMPOSITE VS PRESSURE-TREATED — 10-YEAR COST OF OWNERSHIP

Composite costs roughly 60–75% more on day one. The honest question is not which is cheaper to build — it is which is cheaper to own. Below is the same 20×20 / 400 sq ft deck carried out ten years in Central Massachusetts, where freeze-thaw cycles and winter salt are the real test.

At the ten-year mark pressure-treated is still the cheaper deck, by roughly $6,000–$12,000. The gap closes at year 15–20, when a pressure-treated deck is due for a decking-and-railing resurface ($4,500–$6,500) while the composite is still under warranty. Straight advice from a builder who installs both: if you plan to sell inside 7–10 years, or the deck is small and low to the ground, pressure-treated is the right money. If this is the house you are staying in, the deck is large, or you know you will never get out there with a stain brush every other spring, composite pays for itself in labor you never have to do. Either way, the frame underneath is pressure-treated lumber — the material choice is about the walking surface and railing, not the structure.

WHAT MOVES A DECK PRICE IN MASSACHUSETTS

Six things account for most of the spread inside the ranges above:

  • Height off the ground — a ground-level platform is the cheapest path; second-story decks add $8,000–$18,000 in engineering and structural attachment.
  • Multi-level or wraparound layouts — more framing, more footings, more stair runs.
  • Railing choice — wood is cheapest; composite, aluminum, cable, and glass climb from there ($1,000–$2,000 wood vs $3,000–$5,000 composite on a 16×20).
  • Demolition — tearing off a failing deck before the rebuild adds roughly $1,000–$1,500 on a 16×20.
  • Sloped lots — grade changes add footings and adjustable post systems, common on Central MA hillside and lakefront properties.
  • Footings and frost code — every attached deck needs footings 48 inches below grade in Worcester County; footing count scales with deck size and shape.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

How much does a deck cost in Massachusetts in 2026?

For the projects we quote across Worcester County, a pressure-treated deck (200–400 sq ft) typically runs $8,000–$18,000 installed. A composite deck (300–500 sq ft, Trex or TimberTech) runs $18,000–$35,000. Multi-level or wraparound decks run $28,000–$60,000. Every Maverick City Builders quote is a fixed price in writing and includes the permit, 48-inch frost footings, labor, materials, and cleanup.

What does a 16x20 composite deck cost in Massachusetts?

A new 16x20 (320 sq ft) composite deck on new footings typically runs $16,500–$22,500 installed in Central Massachusetts. A full teardown and rebuild of an existing deck in composite runs $19,000–$26,000. Resurfacing a sound frame with new composite decking and railings runs $12,000–$17,000.

How much cheaper is a pressure-treated deck than composite?

Roughly half the upfront cost — $8,000–$18,000 for pressure-treated versus $18,000–$35,000 for composite at comparable sizes. The tradeoff is maintenance and lifespan: pressure-treated needs staining every 2–3 years and lasts 15–25 years; capped composite needs no staining and carries 25–30+ year lifespans with 25-year surface warranties.

Does the deck price include the permit and footings?

Yes. Every range on this page includes the building permit, footings dug and poured to the 48-inch Worcester County frost depth, the required footing, framing, and final inspections, labor, materials, and standard cleanup. Maverick City Builders pulls the permit and delivers a fixed-price written quote before work starts.

What makes a deck cost more?

The biggest cost drivers are height off the ground, multi-level or wraparound layouts, railing choice (wood is cheapest; composite, aluminum, then cable or glass climb from there), the number of stair runs, demolition of an existing deck, and sloped lots that add footings. A second-story deck or roofline connection adds $8,000–$18,000 for the extra engineering and structural attachment.

How long does a deck project take in Massachusetts?

Most Worcester County building departments issue residential deck permits in 3–5 weeks. After the permit, a standard pressure-treated deck takes 1–2 weeks to build; composite and multi-level decks run 2–4 weeks. Typical total timeline from first call to final inspection is 6–10 weeks.
