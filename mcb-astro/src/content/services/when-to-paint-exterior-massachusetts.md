There is a genuine weather window for exterior painting in Massachusetts, and most of the bad exterior paint jobs in this state were applied outside it.

## The temperature rule, and the part people get wrong

Most exterior coatings specify a minimum application temperature — commonly around 50°F, with some modern acrylics rated down to 35°F.

The part that catches people out is that **it is the surface temperature that matters, not the air temperature, and it matters overnight as well as during the day.**

A coating cures by forming a continuous film. If the surface drops below the minimum while that is still happening, the film does not form properly. It may look fine for a season and then fail early — poor adhesion, chalking, peeling in sheets. A 62°F October afternoon that drops to 38°F overnight is not a painting day, whatever the thermometer said at noon.

Two more that bite in this climate:

- **Dew.** Autumn mornings here produce heavy dew. A coat applied late in the day that gets dewed on before it has skinned over is compromised.
- **Direct sun on a dark wall.** A south-facing dark surface in July can be far hotter than the air. Paint applied to a scorching surface flashes off too fast and does not flow or bond properly. You work around the sun, not through it.

## The Massachusetts calendar

**April** — Marginal. Nights are still dropping into the 30s. Fine for prep, scraping and carpentry repairs. Usually too early to be putting finish coats on.

**May** — The window opens. Reliable daytime temperatures, nights generally staying up. This is a good month and it books out early.

**June, July, August** — Prime season. The constraints are heat and humidity rather than cold: you follow the shade around the house, and high humidity slows drying and can cause problems on humid, still days.

**September** — Excellent. Often the best month of the year here. Warm days, lower humidity than midsummer, nights still mild enough.

**Early to mid October** — Still workable, with attention to the forecast. Dew becomes the limiting factor and the usable part of the day shortens.

**Late October onward** — Done for the year on most products. Cold-weather formulations extend this somewhat, but you are managing risk rather than working comfortably.

**November through March** — Exterior painting season is closed in Central Massachusetts. This is the right time for interior work.

## So when should you actually repaint?

Time since the last paint job is a poor guide. What the surface is doing is the real signal.

**Repaint now:**

- **Chalking.** Run your hand down a sunny wall. If it comes away powdery, the binder has broken down and the coating is at the end of its life.
- **Peeling or flaking**, anywhere.
- **Bare wood showing** at any point. Wood left exposed through a New England winter starts a much more expensive problem.
- **Caulk lines split open** at trim, corners and window surrounds.
- **Widespread fading** with visible colour difference between sheltered and exposed elevations.

**You have time:**

- Colour has dulled but the film is intact and not chalking
- Isolated marks or dirt — wash it and look again
- One elevation weathered and the rest sound. South and west faces here take far more UV and weather than north and east. Painting one elevation is legitimate and much cheaper.

**Stop and investigate first:**

- **Paint peeling in large sheets down to bare wood**, especially on one area rather than generally. That is usually moisture escaping from inside, not a paint failure.
- **Peeling concentrated below a window, a roof line or a gutter.** That is water getting in somewhere. Find the leak first.
- **Soft or spongy wood** anywhere you press on it. That is rot, and paint over rot is money set on fire.

On that last group, painting first just resets the clock on a problem that is still running.

## Why the sequence matters here

In this climate the order is always the same: **fix the water, repair the wood, then paint.** Paint is a finish and a sacrificial layer. It is not a repair, and it is not waterproofing.

We work as a general contractor rather than only a painting outfit, which means when we open up a soft section of trim and find the actual problem, we can price and fix the repair instead of coating over it. That is not always cheaper on the day. It is always cheaper over five years.

## Planning your year

If you are thinking about exterior painting in Central Massachusetts:

- **Book in winter or early spring** for work in May through September. The good weather months fill up.
- **Use autumn and winter for interior work** and for exterior carpentry repairs, which are far less weather-dependent than finish coatings.
- **Let us look at it before the season**, so that if there is rot or a flashing problem behind the failing paint, it is fixed before the paint window opens rather than during it.
