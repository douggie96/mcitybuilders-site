General Contractor in Sterling — Who We Are and What We Build

Ask us for our registration and insurance details in writing and we will provide them directly. Sterling projects tend to be bigger in footprint than what we see in denser towns. Lots are larger, houses have room to grow, and homeowners here are more likely to ask about an addition, a big deck, a garage conversion, or a full first-floor renovation than a single small bath. We do all of those, along with kitchen and bathroom remodels, interior and exterior painting, custom carpentry, flooring, water damage rebuilds, and the structural and rot repair that any house in this climate eventually needs.

We also understand the practical side of working on a rural lot: long driveways for material delivery, well and septic considerations when you add a bathroom or a bedroom, and site conditions that change from one end of town to the other. Those details go into the estimate, not onto a change order later.

Sterling Housing Stock — What We Run Into

Sterling's housing is spread out and varied. Around Sterling Center and along the older stretches of Route 12 and Route 62 you will find antique farmhouses and capes, some with attached barns and ells added over generations, sitting on fieldstone foundations with timber frames. West Sterling and the roads toward Princeton and Leominster have a lot of rural capes and ranches from the 1950s and 1960s on wooded lots. Working on these means dealing with settled sills, old additions that were never properly tied into the main house, and floors that follow the frame rather than a level.

The largest share of Sterling homes we work on are 1970s through 1990s colonials and split-levels built on one- and two-acre lots as the town grew. These houses have conventional framing, full basements, and decent bones, but the original kitchens, baths, and decks are worn out, and a lot of the decks were built before current ledger and footing codes. We have replaced plenty of decks in this age range where the ledger was nailed instead of bolted and the flashing was never installed.

Newer construction from the 2000s onward is scattered through Sterling on larger subdivision lots. Those homes rarely need structural work yet, but owners are starting to upgrade builder-grade kitchens and primary baths, finish basements, and add screened porches and larger composite decks to take advantage of the yard.

What a General Contractor Does vs. Hiring Trades Separately

On a Sterling addition or whole-floor renovation, the number of trades involved gets long: excavation, foundation, framing, roofing, siding, windows, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, insulation, drywall, tile, flooring, trim, and paint. Hiring each one yourself means you are the one calling around when the framer finishes early and the electrician is not free for two weeks, and you are the one who has to know that the insulation inspection has to happen before the drywall goes up.

That coordination is the job of a general contractor. Maverick City Builders writes one contract that covers the whole scope, builds a schedule that puts each trade on site in the right order, pulls the permits through the Sterling Building Department, and manages every inspection. Our own crew handles framing, decks, carpentry, tile, flooring, trim, and paint. Plumbers and electricians handle their portions under our supervision, and we bring in excavation or HVAC subs when the project calls for them.

The result is one price, one timeline, and one person who is responsible for the whole thing. When you hire trades separately and something goes wrong at the seam between two of them, each one points at the other. When you hire a general contractor, that seam is ours, and so is the fix.

How We Work in Sterling

1. Site visit and estimate

We drive out to your property in Sterling, walk the project, take measurements, and talk through what you want and what the house can support. You get a written estimate with an itemized scope and realistic allowances.

2. Fixed-price written contract

The contract is fixed-price and in writing. It lists what is included, what is not, allowances for unknowns behind walls or under decks, the payment schedule, and the timeline. Changes are priced and approved in writing before the work happens.

3. Permits pulled

MCB pulls building, plumbing, and electrical permits through the Sterling Building Department. For additions, we coordinate any septic or zoning questions early so they do not stall the project. We schedule and attend every inspection.

4. One point of contact

One point of contact from the first visit to the final walkthrough. You will not be handed off to a project manager you have never met. The person who wrote your estimate answers the phone.

5. Clean-up and close-out

We clean up daily, protect finished areas and landscaping, and haul debris off site. On larger Sterling lots we plan delivery and dumpster placement to stay off your lawn and septic field. When the job is done, the permit is closed and we walk it with you.
